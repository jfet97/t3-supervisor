# Interface acceptance

Use these product requirements alongside the current PRODUCT.md, DESIGN.md and
surface brief. Keep the existing Operate identity: clear task context, compact
controls, restrained states and useful disclosure. A clarification task does not
authorize a new visual system or unrelated behavior changes.

## Make the first encounter understandable

A contributor's understanding of a term is not evidence that a first-time operator
understands it. For each new product term, unfamiliar state or behavior-changing
control, identify how the user learns its meaning at the point of use. Supply short
associated text, a visible help disclosure, or a specific Guide/glossary link.
Prefer plain wording when the internal term adds no value.

Keep essential meaning visible or explicitly discoverable. A title attribute,
hover-only hint, icon alone or an unlinked entry elsewhere in Guide is insufficient.
Help must work by keyboard and touch. Reuse native HelpDisclosure for guidance and
EntityDetails for inspecting records; keep their purposes distinct. Explain repeated
row statuses once near their list or table rather than adding a paragraph per row.
Keep current state and primary actions outside help disclosures.

Use one maintained vocabulary across control labels, hints, status chips, Guide and
usage skills. Links must reach the relevant topic, with useful focus on entry,
reload and Back. Check the target and explanation, not just the presence of an href.
Do not nest help links inside a row or card that is already a link.

An orphaned chat is retained history tied to a folder confirmed missing at the last
folder check. Unknown, denied or unavailable access does not establish missing.
Explain default project-list hiding, retained Home work/attention and how to recheck.
Do not introduce Home filesystem polling or new visibility rules to simplify copy.

## Preserve navigation and scope

Provide an explicit compact return control for each workspace level, in addition
to breadcrumbs: project pages return to Projects, a conversation returns to its
list, an editor to Definitions, and a template detail to its catalog. Preserve the
appropriate query, page, filters, folder, scroll, drafts and uncertain operations.
Exercise real controls and browser traversal; route round-trip tests alone do not
prove that a visible Back control exists.

Global supervision, project enablement, chat controls, T3 connectivity, native
activity, the Supervisor's participation, plan acceptance and assessed execution
are distinct facts. Never let a green badge or accepted plan claim that work was
performed. Keep stale, incomplete, unknown and unavailable states explicit.
Refresh should name the real read it performs; folder observations are not live
filesystem events. Branch/default/root labels come from actual native facts.

## Review the requested surface

For a whole-interface request, retain a page AND section matrix: what the human
requested, the current observation, the change or justified disposition, and its
acceptance evidence. Include nested views, forms, disclosures, dialogs, empty/error
states and unavailable scopes. A changed-page review cannot justify an all-page
claim. Reconcile each original request with the matrix after the final changes.

Consult available modern-web and accessibility guidance before frontend edits.
Give each heading/copy group and form field an explicit spacing owner. Keep related
text close and leave a larger interval before a separate section. Check computed
gaps in collapsed and expanded states; a late generic stack rule must not override
page-specific rhythm or compress nested forms and history sections.
When Impeccable is installed, read its craft floor immediately before editing;
the acceptance requirements here apply to every contributor. Verify actual renders at desktop and narrow
widths, including 1280 and 320px where these are the affected targets, in both
themes. Check meaningful text, spacing, overflow, focus, keyboard/touch help and
the affected return journeys. Keep existing controls, drafts and state boundaries
intact. Do not replace this work with screenshot counts or permissive assertions.

Record exact source and asset identity for the candidate under review. Distinguish
source observations, synthetic fixture renders, installed behavior and native
execution. Claim screen-reader behavior or automated accessibility results only
when actually measured. A fixture, CI pass or successful deployment proves only
its tested boundary. State known limits without turning missing evidence into a
product bug or inventing a retrospective explanation.

When a reviewer or human corrects a claim, withdraw it explicitly and recheck the
actual artifact. Fix demonstrated failures, then complete the agreed evidence;
do not build another campaign around stale or benign findings. A final complete
claim requires every requested outcome to have a concrete disposition and proof.
